### **Topic 1: Valuing Strategic Flexibility in Health IT Investments: A Real Options Approach to a National Health Data Lake**

- **Research Rationale and Problem Statement:** Public health systems, such as Portugal's National Health Service (NHS), are custodians of vast and growing repositories of longitudinal health data, which are recognized as a "unique source of value".1 To unlock this value, significant, and often irreversible, upfront investments in large-scale data infrastructure—such as a national "Health Data Lake" or "HEALTH DATA SPACE"—are required.2 However, these foundational projects are plagued by profound uncertainty regarding future technological advancements (e.g., the evolution of AI), shifting regulatory landscapes, and the eventual applications and revenue models for secondary data use.3
    
    Traditional capital budgeting methods, particularly Net Present Value (NPV), are ill-equipped for this context. NPV analysis treats the investment as a static, one-time, "now-or-never" decision, forcing all future uncertainties into a single, risk-adjusted discount rate.4 This approach systematically fails to capture the primary source of value in such an investment: the strategic flexibility and future opportunities it creates. Consequently, NPV often assigns a misleadingly low or even negative valuation to these critical infrastructure projects, creating a significant barrier to securing public funding and leading to strategic underinvestment.
    
- **Theoretical Framework:** This research will be grounded in **Real Options Analysis (ROA)**, a valuation methodology derived from financial options theory.6 ROA is designed specifically for valuing investments under high uncertainty where management has the flexibility to adapt its strategy as new information becomes available.8
    
    The core insight is to reframe the investment in a national health data lake not as a single, monolithic project, but as the acquisition of a **portfolio of strategic growth options**.10 The initial capital outlay is conceptualized as the "option premium" paid to secure the right, but not the obligation, to undertake various future data-driven initiatives.4 This framework allows for the explicit quantification of managerial flexibility—the value of being able to expand, defer, switch, or even abandon sub-projects as the future unfolds.7
    
- **Key Research Questions:**
    
    1. **How can the "underlying asset" of a public health data lake be defined and valued?** This involves modeling the present value of all potential future value streams the platform could enable, including operational efficiencies, cost savings from improved public health interventions, and potential revenue from regulated, secondary use of anonymized data in clinical trials and research.13
        
    2. **What are the key real options embedded in a national health data infrastructure investment, and how can they be valued?** This research would identify and model a series of options, including:
        
        - **The Option to Expand:** The value of being able to scale the platform to incorporate new data sources (e.g., genomic data, data from wearable devices) or new analytical capabilities (e.g., next-generation AI tools) as these technologies mature.7
            
        - **The Option to Defer:** The value of being able to delay investment in specific, high-cost applications (e.g., a nationwide predictive analytics system) until there is greater technological certainty or clearer evidence of its value.10
            
        - **The Option to Partner (Growth Option):** The value of creating a platform that enables future public-private partnerships for research and development, which would not be possible without the initial investment.11
            
    3. **How does incorporating the value of these real options change the overall valuation of the health data lake project compared to a traditional NPV analysis?**
        
- **Proposed Methodology:** A mixed-methods approach would be employed.
    
    - **Quantitative Modeling:** The primary methodology will be the development of a Real Options valuation model, likely using a **Binomial Tree or Black-Scholes framework**.6 This will involve estimating the key parameters for the model: the value of the underlying asset, the investment costs (exercise prices), the timeframe of the options (time to expiration), the volatility of the asset's value (a proxy for uncertainty), and the risk-free interest rate.7 Data for these inputs will be derived from public health financial reports, technology market forecasts, and econometric analysis of historical health data.
        
    - **Qualitative Analysis:** To ensure the model's relevance and to validate its assumptions, in-depth case studies and semi-structured interviews will be conducted with key stakeholders. This would include policymakers at the Ministry of Health, hospital CFOs, and health IT leaders within a system like the Portuguese NHS. This qualitative work is essential for correctly identifying the most relevant real options and understanding the practical constraints and triggers for exercising them.
        
- **Expected Contribution:**
    
    - **For Theory:** This research would provide a rigorous application of ROA to a novel and critical domain: large-scale public sector digital infrastructure. It extends the use of real options from its traditional applications in private R&D and natural resources to the complex world of public health policy and investment.15
        
    - **For Practice:** The primary contribution is a practical and theoretically sound decision-making tool for governments and health authorities. By providing a credible financial valuation of strategic flexibility, this framework would offer a powerful counter-argument to short-term, cost-focused budgeting. It would enable policymakers to make more strategically astute capital allocation decisions, justifying the foundational investments necessary to build a truly data-driven, value-based healthcare system.3
