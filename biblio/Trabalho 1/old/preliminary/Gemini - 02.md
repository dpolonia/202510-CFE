### **Topic 2: Beyond Cost Savings: A Multi-Attribute Framework for Valuing a Public Health Data Repository as a Strategic Asset**

- **Research Rationale and Problem Statement:** National health systems, such as Portugal's, are amassing vast, comprehensive, and longitudinal health data repositories. These are widely recognized as a "unique source of value" with immense potential to improve public health and drive innovation.1 However, the economic value of this data is poorly understood and rarely quantified beyond simple cost-avoidance calculations. This chronic undervaluation results in a critical lack of investment in the very infrastructure needed to realize its potential: robust data governance, quality assurance, interoperability, and security. Without a credible and comprehensive valuation methodology, policymakers struggle to justify the substantial, long-term investments required to transform this raw data into a functioning strategic national asset.
    
- **Theoretical Framework:** This research will be grounded in the corporate finance literature on the **valuation of intangible assets**.3 An intangible asset, such as a brand, a patent, or a data repository, lacks physical form but can possess enormous economic value. Because no single method can capture the multifaceted value of public health data, this study will develop a **hybrid, multi-attribute valuation framework**. This framework will integrate several established approaches:
    
    - **Income Approach:** This method values the asset based on the future economic benefits it is expected to generate. This includes quantifiable streams like cost savings from more efficient, data-driven public health interventions and potential revenue from the regulated, secondary use of anonymized data for accelerating clinical trials.2
        
    - **Market Approach:** This involves benchmarking the asset against similar assets that have been part of a market transaction. While direct public-sector comparisons are scarce, this could involve analyzing private-sector health data company valuations or the terms of public-private data-sharing partnerships.
        
    - **Cost Approach:** This establishes a baseline value by estimating the cost to recreate the asset. For a decades-long, nationwide longitudinal health database, this replacement cost would be astronomical, highlighting the irreplaceability of the existing asset.
        
    - **Non-Financial Valuation (Multi-Attribute Utility Theory):** Recognizing that a significant portion of the data's value is social rather than purely financial (e.g., improved health equity, enhanced pandemic preparedness, fostering a domestic innovation ecosystem), the framework will incorporate non-financial metrics, similar to a balanced scorecard, to quantify this broader societal return.5
        
- **Key Research Questions:**
    
    1. What are the distinct value-generating capabilities of a national health data repository? This research will seek to model the value derived from its use in four key areas:
        
        - **(a) Accelerating Clinical Research:** Quantifying the economic value of reducing the time and cost of clinical trials and enabling more robust real-world evidence studies.
            
        - **(b) Enhancing Public Health Intelligence:** Valuing the asset's contribution to improved disease surveillance, more effective policy-making, and optimized resource allocation.
            
        - **(c) Enabling Personalized Medicine:** Assessing the long-term option value of having the foundational data infrastructure required to deliver precision medicine at a national scale.
            
        - **(d) Catalyzing a Health-Tech Innovation Ecosystem:** Valuing the economic impact of making curated, anonymized data accessible to researchers and startups to foster domestic innovation.
            
    2. How can these diverse value streams—encompassing both direct financial returns and broader societal benefits—be integrated into a single, coherent, and defensible valuation model?
        
    3. What is the "value gap" between this comprehensive valuation and a narrow, cost-savings-only assessment, and what are the implications for public investment strategy in national data infrastructure?
        
- **Proposed Methodology:**
    
    - **Framework Construction:** The initial phase will involve a systematic review of intangible asset valuation literature and its applications in both the public and private healthcare sectors to build the theoretical structure of the hybrid model.
        
    - **Quantitative Financial Modeling:** For the income and market-based components, the research will employ discounted cash flow (DCF) analysis and comparable transactions analysis. This will require gathering data on the economic burden of specific diseases, the typical costs and timelines of clinical trials, and market rates for health data licensing.
        
    - **Qualitative Analysis and Expert Elicitation:** To quantify the non-financial and highly uncertain components of value, the research will use qualitative methods. This will involve conducting **Delphi panels** and semi-structured interviews with a diverse group of experts, including health economists, public health officials, data scientists, clinical researchers, and leaders from the pharmaceutical and health-tech industries. Their consensus judgments will be used to assign weights and scores to the non-financial attributes of the valuation framework. A case study focusing on Portugal's "HEALTH DATA SPACE" initiative would provide a concrete context for this analysis.1
        
- **Expected Contribution:**
    
    - **For Theory:** This study would pioneer a comprehensive, theoretically robust framework for valuing a public health data repository as a strategic intangible asset, significantly advancing the literature on public sector finance and the valuation of non-traditional assets.
        
    - **For Practice:** The research will deliver a powerful and practical tool for governments and public health agencies. A credible, multi-attribute valuation provides the robust financial and social business case needed to justify major public investment in data infrastructure. It also creates an analytical foundation for designing data governance and access policies that maximize the asset's total value to society while safeguarding public trust and ensuring compliance with privacy regulations.2
