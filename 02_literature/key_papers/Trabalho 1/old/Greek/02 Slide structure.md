## SLIDE 7: THEORETICAL CONTRIBUTION - What's Actually Offered

### 💎 **The One Genuine Insight: "Transferred Distress"**

**Concept:** Public hospitals don't experience distress themselves (state bailout), but **transfer it to stakeholders:**

```
┌─────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│   Traditional Financial Distress (Private)  │
│   Firm → Bankruptcy → Stakeholders lose     │
└─────────────────────────────────────────────┘

┌─────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│   "Transferred Distress" (Public)           │
│   Entity survives ← State absorbs costs     │
│   ↓                                          │
│   Suppliers: 1,485-day payment delays       │
│   Taxpayers: €1.9B annual subsidies         │
│   Patients: Potential quality deterioration  │
└─────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

**Problem:** This insight remains **underdeveloped**

## SLIDE 8: METHODOLOGICAL RIGOR - Three Fatal Flaws

### 🚨 **FLAW 1: Wrong Tool for the Job**

**Altman's Z″-Score designed to predict:**

- **Bankruptcy probability** in next 1-2 years
- For **private service firms**
- In **competitive markets**
- Validated on sample with actual failures

**Applied to:**

- **Public hospitals** where bankruptcy is impossible by design
- **Zero observed failures** in sample
- **Soft budget constraints** with continuous state support

> "Although no bankruptcies or closures of these entities have been reported to date" (p. 2)

**Result:** **No validation criterion** - Can't test if model works because outcome never occurs

## SLIDE 10: METHODOLOGICAL RIGOR - The Circular Reasoning Trap

### 🚨 **FLAW 3: Measurement Validity Paradox**

**Paper's Argument Chain:**

```
Step 1: Use Z-scores to measure distress ✓
         ↓
Step 2: Find Z-scores are high → No distress ✓
         ↓
Step 3: Argue Z-scores are invalid because:
        • X1 (working capital) overstates liquidity
          (93.5% is receivables collected in 1,485 days)
        • X4 (equity/liability) understates leverage
          (equity is mostly subsidies, not retained earnings) ✓
         ↓
Step 4: Conclude hospitals ARE distressed...
        ...but Z-scores don't show it ❓
```

**The Problem:** Can't use an invalid measure to test hypotheses (H1-H5), then declare it invalid!

**Methodological Grade: WEAK-ADEQUATE (2.5/5)**

## SLIDE 18: TRANSFORMATION ROADMAP (Continued)

#### **3. DEVELOP ALTERNATIVE DISTRESS MEASURE**

**Current:** Relies solely on Z-scores, admits they're invalid

**Needed: "Public Hospital Financial Sustainability Index (PHFSI)"**

**Components (equally weighted):**

```
1. Operational Self-Sufficiency Ratio
   = Operating Revenue / (Operating Expenses - Subsidies)
   
2. Stakeholder Pressure Index
   = (Accounts Payable Days / 90) + (Overdue Liabilities / Total Liabilities)
   
3. Receivables Realization Rate
   = Cash Collections / Accrued Revenue (adjusted for lag)
   
4. Subsidy Dependency Ratio
   = State Subsidies / Total Revenue
   
5. Clinical Quality Maintenance Index
   = (Patient Safety Score + Access Metrics) / Benchmarks
```

**Validate by:**

- Showing low PHFSI predicts government intervention
- Demonstrating PHFSI captures problems Z-scores miss
- Testing across multiple countries/time periods

## SLIDE 24: KEY TAKEAWAYS - The Five Critical Lessons

### 🎯 **1. CONTEXT MATTERS - Model Validity is Conditional**

**Lesson:** Altman's Z-score works for private firms in competitive markets with bankruptcy risk. It **fails catastrophically** when:

- Bankruptcy is impossible (government guarantee)
- "Equity" is subsidies, not retained earnings
- "Liabilities" exclude debt (no access to capital markets)
- "Working capital" is uncollectible receivables

**Implication:** Always interrogate model assumptions before application

---

### 🎯 **2. DESCRIPTION ≠ EXPLANATION**

**Paper does:** Documents patterns (size matters, gender matters, location matters)

**Paper doesn't do:** Explains WHY using theory

**Lesson:** Advanced CF requires **causal mechanisms**, not just correlations

---

### 🎯 **3. CIRCULAR REASONING TRAP**

**The Logic Flaw:**

```
"We'll measure X with tool Y"
    ↓
"Tool Y says no X exists"
    ↓
"But tool Y is invalid"
    ↓
"Therefore X exists"  ⚠️ INVALID!
```

**Lesson:** Can't use an invalid measure to support claims—must develop valid alternative FIRST

---

### 🎯 **4. VALIDATION REQUIRES OUTCOMES**

**Prediction models need:**

- Sample with both successes and failures
- Observation period to see predictions realized
- Clear definition of outcome being predicted

**This paper has:** Zero failures, one time point, unclear outcome definition

**Lesson:** Can't validate without variation in the thing you're predicting

---

### 🎯 **5. THEORETICAL PROMISE vs. DELIVERY**

**Claiming to "redefine" something requires:**

1. Clear new definition
2. Theoretical justification
3. Operational measures
4. Empirical validation

**This paper provides:** Only #1 (partially)

**Lesson:** Don't overpromise theoretical contributions; deliver what you claim or claim what you deliver

### A1: Detailed Z-Score Formula Breakdown

**Altman's Z″-Score Model:**

$$Z = 6.56X_1 + 3.26X_2 + 6.72X_3 + 1.05X_4$$

Where:

- $X_1 = \frac{\text{Working Capital}}{\text{Total Assets}}$ (Liquidity)
- $X_2 = \frac{\text{Retained Earnings}}{\text{Total Assets}}$ (Profitability History)
- $X_3 = \frac{\text{EBIT}}{\text{Total Assets}}$ (Operating Efficiency)
- $X_4 = \frac{\text{Book Equity}}{\text{Total Liabilities}}$ (Leverage/Solvency)

**Cutoff Interpretation:**

- **Z < 1.10:** Distress zone (high bankruptcy probability)
- **1.10 ≤ Z ≤ 2.60:** Gray zone (uncertain)
- **Z > 2.60:** Safe zone (low bankruptcy probability)

### A3: Capital Structure Theory - Application to Public Hospitals

**Pecking Order Theory (Myers 1984):**

**Prediction:** Firms prefer internal financing > debt > equity (due to information asymmetry)

**Application to Public Hospitals:**

```
Preference Hierarchy:
1. Government subsidies (analogous to "internal" from state owner)
2. Retained earnings (very limited)
3. Debt (NOT USED - would require market discipline)
4. Equity issuance (IMPOSSIBLE - no shares)

Result: Zero leverage (observed)
```

**Trade-Off Theory (Kraus & Litzenberger 1973):**

**Prediction:** Optimal leverage balances tax shields vs. financial distress costs

**Application to Public Hospitals:**

```
Tax Shield Benefits: ZERO (public entities don't pay taxes)
Financial Distress Costs: VERY HIGH (threatens public mission)

Optimal Leverage: ZERO ✓

Result: No debt (observed)
```

**Conclusion:** Both theories predict the observed capital structure!  
**Paper's Missed Opportunity:** Could have used theory to explain, not just describe

### A4: Statistical Power Analysis - Example

**H4: Hospital Specialty Affects Z-scores**

**Sample Composition:**

- General hospitals: n=68
- General + health centers: n=7
- University hospitals: n=7
- Anticancer hospitals: n=2 ⚠️
- Other: n=6

**Kruskal-Wallis Test Result:** H = 2.275, p = 0.685 (not significant)

**But:** With only 2 anticancer hospitals, statistical power to detect a true difference is:

$$\text{Power} \approx 0.15 \text{ (15%)}$$

**Interpretation:** 85% chance of Type II error (false negative)

**Conclusion:** Null finding may reflect inadequate power, not absence of effect

**Paper's Flaw:** Accepts null hypothesis without discussing power
