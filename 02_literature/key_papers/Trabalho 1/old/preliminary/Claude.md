# Ten PhD Research Topics: Data Use in Value-Based Healthcare and Advanced Corporate Finance

## Topic 1: Redefining Financial Distress in Beveridgean Healthcare Systems Through Real-Time Data Analytics

**Research Gap Addressed**: Traditional financial distress prediction models (Altman Z-score, Ohlson O-score) fail catastrophically in public healthcare contexts due to the "continuous state-administered bailout" phenomenon. Greek hospital research reveals that conventional metrics show apparent viability while hospitals impose severe financial pressure on suppliers and taxpayers, with receivables collected after 1,485 days on average.

**Innovative Approach**: Develop a novel **Dynamic Stakeholder Financial Pressure Index (DSFPI)** that integrates real-time data from multiple sources: supplier payment velocity, government subsidy dependence ratios, clinical outcome deterioration markers, workforce retention metrics, and patient access indicators. Unlike static annual models, this framework uses machine learning on streaming healthcare data to predict **mission-critical financial distress** (inability to fulfill healthcare mission) rather than bankruptcy, which is irrelevant for public entities.

**Connection to Corporate Finance**: Directly applies **financial distress & bankruptcy prediction** and **empirical methods in corporate finance** (GMM, panel data analysis, event study methodology) from the syllabus, but fundamentally reconceptualizes the dependent variable from "probability of bankruptcy" to "probability of mission failure requiring emergency intervention."

**Relevant Q1 Journals**:
- *Journal of Health Economics* (health economics and financial sustainability)
- *Health Care Management Science* (quantitative methods and analytics)
- *Healthcare Analytics* (predictive modeling and real-time data applications)

## Topic 2: Real Options Valuation for Value-Based Care Transformation Investments in Tax-Funded Systems

**Research Gap Addressed**: Capital budgeting in Beveridgean systems faces a fundamental mismatch: value-based care requires multi-year investments with uncertain payoffs, but annual budget cycles and political pressures demand short-term justification. Traditional NPV analysis fails to capture the strategic value of flexibility inherent in staged VBC implementation. The Swedish cardiac monitoring example illustrates how budget fragmentation prevents value-creating investments even when technology is inexpensive.

**Innovative Approach**: Apply **real options theory** to VBC transformation, modeling each implementation stage (data infrastructure, analytics capabilities, payment model transition, care redesign) as **compound options**. The framework values: (1) **option to defer** until better outcome data emerges, (2) **option to expand** from pilot to system-wide implementation, (3) **option to switch** between payment models, and (4) **option to abandon** unsuccessful pathways. Critically, incorporate **cross-budget option value** where upfront investor (e.g., primary care) differs from savings beneficiary (e.g., hospital), creating novel "split-beneficiary option" valuation requiring coordination mechanisms.

**Connection to Corporate Finance**: Applies **advanced valuation techniques** (real options, contingent claims analysis) and **capital budgeting** from syllabus, extending theory to multi-period, multi-stakeholder, mission-constrained contexts with misaligned financial incentives.

**Relevant Q1 Journals**:
- *Strategic Management Journal* (strategic investment under uncertainty)
- *Journal of Health Economics* (healthcare investment evaluation)
- *Health Care Management Science* (operations research and decision analysis)

## Topic 3: Data Governance as Capital Structure: Designing Optimal Information Architectures for Healthcare Financial Performance

**Research Gap Addressed**: Healthcare organizations invest heavily in data infrastructure (Nordic countries have world-leading registries, yet data utilization remains limited), but no framework exists to determine **optimal data governance "capital structure"** balancing accessibility (analogous to financial leverage) against privacy/security risks (analogous to bankruptcy costs). Current 70% of organizations lack comprehensive data strategies, representing massive underutilization of valuable "data capital."

**Innovative Approach**: Develop a **Data Governance Trade-Off Theory** parallel to capital structure theory. Model data accessibility as leverage: greater accessibility increases "returns" (innovation, efficiency, research value) but increases "distress costs" (breach risk, compliance costs, loss of trust). Use Nordic registry data linking governance structures to: (1) innovation outputs (publications, startups), (2) operational efficiency gains, (3) breach incidents, and (4) regulatory penalties. Identify optimal "data leverage ratios" and test pecking order predictions (organizations prefer internal analytics over external partnerships, analogous to internal financing preference).

**Connection to Corporate Finance**: Directly extends **capital structure theory** (trade-off theory, pecking order theory, financial distress costs) and **corporate governance** from syllabus to data assets, creating novel theoretical contribution treating information as financial capital requiring optimal structure.

**Relevant Q1 Journals**:
- *Academy of Management Journal* (theory development in organizational resources)
- *Journal of Healthcare Informatics Research* (data governance frameworks)
- *Health Care Management Science* (analytical modeling of governance structures)

## Topic 5: M&A Valuation with Integrated Clinical-Financial Data: A Study of NHS Trust Consolidations

**Research Gap Addressed**: Hospital mergers and consolidations in Beveridgean systems (Denmark reduced from 14 to 5 regions) lack valuation frameworks integrating clinical outcome data with financial metrics. Traditional M&A valuation focuses on cost synergies and revenue enhancement, but public healthcare M&A must demonstrate **clinical value preservation** and **equity maintenance** alongside financial benefits. Current practice applies corporate M&A tools without adaptation, leading to value destruction through clinical quality degradation.

**Innovative Approach**: Develop **Dual-Objective M&A Valuation Framework** for public healthcare using **adjusted present value (APV)** methodology extended with **clinical-financial value linkage**. Core innovation: estimate **clinical quality synergies** (care pathway integration, standardized protocols, reduced variation) separately from financial synergies, then model their interaction. Use population health registries (Nordic countries) or NHS Hospital Episode Statistics to measure actual post-merger clinical outcomes (readmissions, complications, mortality) and link to financial performance through time-series regression with synthetic control methodology. Incorporate **equity preservation constraints** as minimum thresholds rather than objectives.

**Connection to Corporate Finance**: Applies **M&A valuation methodologies** (APV, synergy valuation, due diligence) and **valuation techniques** (DCF, enterprise value) from syllabus, innovating by treating clinical outcomes as quantifiable value drivers requiring distinct measurement and integration with financial metrics.

**Relevant Q1 Journals**:
- *Strategic Management Journal* (M&A research and organizational integration)
- *Health Care Management Science* (quantitative healthcare operations)
- *Medical Care Research and Review* (health services research on organizational change)

## Topic 6: Financial Engineering for Cross-Budget Value Capture in Integrated Care Systems

**Research Gap Addressed**: Fundamental barrier to value-based healthcare in Beveridgean systems is **budget fragmentation**: municipalities fund social care, regions fund hospitals, national government funds primary care, creating misaligned incentives. Swedish cardiac monitoring example: local clinic pays €5,000 upfront, regional hospital saves €50,000 annually, neither will fund. No financial instruments exist to capture value across budget silos, preventing optimal societal investments.

**Innovative Approach**: Design **Cross-Budget Value Bonds (CVBs)** - structured financial instruments similar to asset-backed securities that securitize future savings from preventive interventions. Use **special purpose vehicles (SPVs)** to issue bonds backed by actuarially predicted savings streams from integrated care programs (e.g., diabetes prevention). Bondholders receive returns from realized savings, measured through population health registries. Innovation: **data-driven tranching** where senior tranches receive priority claims on high-confidence savings (reduced hospitalizations), junior tranches capture uncertain longer-term benefits (workforce productivity, reduced disability). Requires **convertible bond** features allowing conversion from fixed savings targets to equity-like participation in upside if intervention exceeds projections.

**Connection to Corporate Finance**: Applies **structured finance & complex financing** (asset-backed securities, SPVs, convertible bonds), **financial contracting** (security design, covenant design), and **risk management** (tranching, risk allocation) from syllabus to solve unique public sector coordination failure.

**Relevant Q1 Journals**:
- *Journal of Health Economics* (health financing innovation)
- *Health Affairs* (health policy and payment innovation)
- *Strategic Management Journal* (inter-organizational value creation)

## Topic 8: Risk-Adjusted Cost of Capital for Value-Based Care Contracts in Single-Payer Systems

**Research Gap Addressed**: Value-based care contracts shift financial risk from payers to providers (hospitals, physician groups), but **no consensus exists on appropriate risk premiums** for VBC arrangements in Beveridgean systems. Unlike private insurance markets with observable risk prices, single-payer systems lack market mechanisms for risk pricing. Finnish experts note annual budgets incompatible with high upfront cost + long payback models, suggesting **structural cost of capital barriers** specific to tax-funded systems. Current practice: arbitrary discount rates or government bond rates that ignore systematic risk.

**Innovative Approach**: Estimate **Healthcare Value-Based Care Asset Pricing Model (HVB-CAPM)** using cross-country panel data from NHS and Nordic integrated care systems. Treat VBC contracts as quasi-equity instruments with uncertain "returns" (savings relative to targets). Estimate beta coefficients measuring correlation between VBC contract outcomes and macroeconomic factors (GDP growth, healthcare expenditure growth, demographic shifts, epidemic shocks). Core innovation: decompose cost of capital into **base rate** (government borrowing), **clinical risk premium** (outcome uncertainty), **operational risk premium** (care redesign execution), and **political risk premium** (payment model stability across election cycles). Use GLM and GMM methodologies to estimate risk premiums from observed VBC contract pricing and performance data.

**Connection to Corporate Finance**: Directly applies **cost of capital** (CAPM, risk-return relationships), **empirical methods** (GMM, panel data analysis), and **risk management** (risk assessment, systematic vs. idiosyncratic risk) from syllabus to healthcare contracting context.

**Relevant Q1 Journals**:
- *Journal of Health Economics* (health economics and financial risk)
- *Health Care Management Science* (quantitative methods in healthcare finance)
- *American Journal of Health Economics* (in-depth health economic analysis)

## Research Design Considerations

Each topic incorporates:

1. **Quantitative rigor**: Econometric identification strategies (DID, synthetic control, instrumental variables, GMM, panel data methods)

2. **Data sources**: Nordic population registries, NHS Hospital Episode Statistics, financial statements from public hospitals, VBC contract data, ICB allocations

3. **Theoretical contribution**: Extends corporate finance theory to unique healthcare contexts, generating new predictions and insights

4. **Practical relevance**: Addresses real barriers facing healthcare leaders in Beveridgean systems

5. **Policy implications**: Provides actionable frameworks for healthcare finance and data governance policy
